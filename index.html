Abstract

FoundationPose++ improves 6D object pose tracking by combining FoundationPose with Kalman-filter-based temporal prediction and an additional 2D tracker. Its original filter represents orientation using XYZ Euler angles and performs prediction and correction directly in Euclidean coordinates. This project reformulates the temporal filtering component as an error-state Extended Kalman Filter on \(SE(3)\), keeping the nominal rigid-body pose on the group while representing local pose errors and corrections in the tangent space.

Under smooth simulated motion, the Euler-angle KF and the proposed \(SE(3)\) ES-EKF achieve nearly identical pose accuracy. Targeted real-world stress tests, however, expose representation-specific limitations of the Euler formulation: continuous rotations across the \(\pm180^\circ\) branch boundary produce near-\(360^\circ\) coordinate residuals, while poses approaching the XYZ Euler \(\pm90^\circ\) middle-angle singularity produce strongly coupled rotational coordinates that propagate into the filter innovation and state. The \(SE(3)\) formulation instead represents rotational discrepancies as local group-consistent errors, avoiding these Euler-coordinate discontinuities and singularities.

Repeated real-time RGB-D trials further demonstrate sustained operation of the complete \(SE(3)\) pipeline at approximately \(30\) FPS. The contribution is therefore a geometrically consistent and representation-robust formulation of rigid-body pose filtering rather than a universal improvement in pose accuracy.

Motivation

FoundationPose++ introduces temporal motion prediction through a Kalman filter, but represents the 6D object pose using translation and XYZ Euler angles in a Euclidean state vector. While this representation is simple and effective for moderate motion, rotations do not naturally form a Euclidean vector space.

In particular, Euler-angle coordinates can exhibit discontinuities and singular configurations. For example, two orientations represented by \(179^\circ\) and \(-179^\circ\) are physically only about \(2^\circ\) apart, while direct subtraction of their Euler coordinates gives a difference close to \(358^\circ\). Similarly, for XYZ Euler angles, configurations with pitch approaching \(\pm 90^\circ\) become singular, making the coordinate representation highly sensitive to small physical changes in orientation.

\[ 179^\circ \rightarrow -179^\circ \qquad \text{small physical rotation, large coordinate change} \]

These are representation effects rather than failures of the underlying rigid-body motion. A rigid-body pose naturally belongs to the Lie group \(SE(3)\), suggesting that the nominal pose should remain on the group instead of being represented and corrected directly through Euler coordinates.

Euler-angle KF \( [\mathbf p,\boldsymbol{\theta}] \in \mathbb R^6 \) Pose prediction and correction in Euclidean coordinates
→
SE(3) ES-EKF \( \mathbf T \in SE(3), \qquad \delta\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\mathfrak{se}(3) \) Nominal pose on the group, local errors in the tangent space
Motivation: preserve the geometry of rigid-body motion by representing the nominal pose directly on \(SE(3)\), while retaining a locally Euclidean error state for Kalman filtering.

Method

FoundationPose++

FoundationPose++ combines FoundationPose with Kalman-filter-based temporal motion prediction and an additional 2D tracker. Its per-frame tracking pipeline is preserved in this project.

6D Pose Update → 2D Tracker Update → FoundationPose Refinement → Motion Prediction

The original Kalman filter uses the Euclidean state \[ \mathbf{x}_k = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{p}_k & \boldsymbol{\theta}_k & \mathbf{v}_k & \dot{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_k \end{bmatrix}^{T} \in \mathbb{R}^{12}, \] where \(\boldsymbol{\theta}_k\) denotes XYZ Euler angles.

\(SE(3)\) Error-State EKF

The nominal rigid-body pose remains on \(SE(3)\), while the EKF estimates only local errors in the tangent space. A left-multiplicative pose-error convention is used for correction.

1

Pose Residual

The relative pose between the FoundationPose observation and the predicted pose is mapped to a local residual:

\[ \mathbf r_k = \operatorname{Log}_{SE(3)} \left( \mathbf T_{\mathrm{obs}} (\mathbf T_k^-)^{-1} \right). \]
2

EKF Update

The local residual is used to compute the Kalman gain and estimate the error-state correction. Here, \(\delta\hat{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_k\) denotes the 6D pose-error component of the full error-state correction \(\delta\hat{\mathbf{x}}_k\):

\[ \mathbf S_k = \mathbf H_k\mathbf P_k^-\mathbf H_k^T+\mathbf R_k \] \[ \mathbf K_k = \mathbf P_k^-\mathbf H_k^T\mathbf S_k^{-1}, \qquad \delta\hat{\mathbf x}_k = \mathbf K_k\mathbf r_k, \qquad \delta\hat{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_k = \left[\delta\hat{\mathbf x}_k\right]_{1:6} \]
3

\(SE(3)\) Injection

The estimated pose-error component is mapped back to the group and injected into the nominal pose:

\[ \mathbf T_k^+ = \operatorname{Exp}_{SE(3)} \left(\delta\hat{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_k\right) \mathbf T_k^-. \]
4

Prediction

The corrected pose and covariance are propagated to the next frame using a constant-spatial-velocity model. The spatial motion vector \(\mathbf{u}_k=[\mathbf{v}_k^T,\boldsymbol{\omega}_k^T]^T\) contains the linear and angular velocities expressed in the camera/world frame:

\[ \mathbf T_{k+1}^- = \operatorname{Exp}_{SE(3)} \left(\mathbf u_k\Delta t_k\right) \mathbf T_k^+ \] \[ \mathbf P_{k+1}^- = \mathbf F_k\mathbf P_k^+\mathbf F_k^T+\mathbf Q_k \]
Key idea: \(SE(3)\) handles the geometry of the nominal rigid-body pose, while the EKF estimates local errors and their uncertainty in Euclidean coordinates.

Experiment III — Real-Time Real-World Tracking

The real-world experiments use a ZED RGB-D camera with OSTrack as the additional 2D measurement source. They first evaluate repeated real-time \(SE(3)\) ES-EKF tracking with two objects, a mustard bottle and a banana, and then compare the behaviour of the Euler-angle KF and the \(SE(3)\) ES-EKF under targeted rotational conditions.

A. Repeated Real-Time Tracking

The proposed \(SE(3)\) ES-EKF was evaluated through repeated real-time RGB-D tracking runs using two objects: a mustard bottle and a banana. Three approximately two-minute real-time tracking videos were recorded for each object, giving six trials of comparable duration. These trials evaluate the sustained online operation of the complete \(SE(3)\) tracking pipeline under ordinary manual object motion.

B. Continuous Multi-Turn Rotation across the Euler Branch Boundary

The object was rotated continuously in one direction for approximately two complete turns. Separate recordings were collected for the original Euler-angle KF and the proposed \(SE(3)\) ES-EKF. The recordings are therefore independent trials rather than frame-synchronized executions of identical motion.

Euler-angle KF

Two-turn Euler-angle KF trial with two branch-boundary crossings.

The tracking video is synchronized with the Euler \(r_z\) trajectory. During the continuous two-turn rotation, two branch-crossing events are observed as the Euler representation passes the \(\pm180^\circ\) boundary.

Here, \(r_z\) denotes the third component of the XYZ Euler-angle representation \((r_x,r_y,r_z)\) of the object orientation \(^{C}\mathbf{R}_{O}\) with respect to the camera frame. It is a wrapped orientation coordinate, not the accumulated physical rotation angle of the object. Therefore, even though the object rotates continuously through multiple complete turns, \(r_z\) remains on its selected Euler branch and can repeatedly switch between values near \(+180^\circ\) and \(-180^\circ\).

In the independent \(SE(3)\) ES-EKF trial, no corresponding branch-boundary discontinuity was observed, since the orientation was not represented by wrapped Euler-angle coordinates.

\(SE(3)\) ES-EKF

Independent two-turn trial using the \(SE(3)\) error-state formulation.

Why does the Euler branch crossing matter?

The orientation of the object is represented by \(^{C}\mathbf{R}_{O}\), and the original filter converts this rotation matrix to XYZ Euler coordinates \((r_x,r_y,r_z)\). During continuous rotation, the third Euler component can cross its representation boundary even though the physical orientation remains continuous. At the first detected crossing, the predicted and observed coordinates were

\[ r_z^{\mathrm{pred}} = +179.71^\circ, \qquad r_z^{\mathrm{obs}} = -178.28^\circ. \]

The observed value is an equivalent wrapped representation: \(-178.28^\circ \equiv 181.72^\circ\). Thus, the underlying orientation continues smoothly across the boundary. However, the original linear Kalman filter treats the Euler coordinates as ordinary Euclidean variables and computes the innovation by direct subtraction:

\[ r_z^{\mathrm{obs}}-r_z^{\mathrm{pred}} = -178.28^\circ-179.71^\circ = -357.99^\circ. \]

The resulting large residual is therefore a coordinate artifact rather than a physical \(357.99^\circ\) inter-frame rotation.

Effect on the Kalman correction

In the original Euler-angle KF, this innovation is not only a diagnostic quantity; it directly enters the state correction through

\[ \hat{\mathbf{x}}_{k|k} = \hat{\mathbf{x}}_{k|k-1} + \mathbf{K}_k\mathbf{y}_k . \]

Therefore, a near-\(360^\circ\) Euler residual can produce a large correction to the rotational state, depending on the corresponding Kalman gain. The final output pose does not undergo the full \(360^\circ\) change because the innovation is weighted by \(\mathbf{K}_k\), and the corrected pose is subsequently processed by the pose-refinement stage. Nevertheless, the detected branch-crossing frames coincide with pronounced transient changes in the final estimated orientation.

In contrast, the \(SE(3)\) formulation compares orientations through the local relative rotation,

\[ \boldsymbol{\phi}_k = \operatorname{Log}_{SO(3)} \left( \mathbf{R}_{\mathrm{obs}} \mathbf{R}_{\mathrm{pred}}^{T} \right), \]

so the rotational innovation is expressed locally on the Lie algebra rather than by directly subtracting Euler-angle coordinates.

C. Near-\(90^\circ\) Euler Singularity

A second real-world stress test examines the XYZ Euler singularity that occurs as the middle XYZ Euler coordinate \(r_y\) approaches \(\pm90^\circ\). Two complementary tests are used. First, dynamic trials move the object through the near-singular region to examine tracking behaviour. Second, a controlled stationary-object trial isolates how the Euler-coordinate representation affects the internal Kalman-filter quantities.

Why does the near-\(90^\circ\) configuration matter?

In the XYZ Euler representation, the parameterization becomes singular as the middle XYZ Euler coordinate \(r_y\) approaches \(\pm90^\circ\), where \(\cos(r_y)\) approaches zero. Near this configuration, the two outer Euler coordinates \(r_x\) and \(r_z\) become strongly coupled and are no longer independently well-conditioned. Consequently, a small and smooth change in the physical orientation can produce large, coupled changes in the Euler-coordinate representation.

Dynamic Near-Singularity Stress Test

Four dynamic trials were recorded with the Euler-angle KF while the object orientation passed through a near-singular configuration. All four recordings subsequently exhibited tracking loss. Two independent \(SE(3)\) ES-EKF stress-test recordings maintained tracking. Because these recordings are independent rather than frame-synchronized executions of identical motion, the numerical values below characterize the observed behaviour of each formulation rather than constitute a paired accuracy benchmark.

Controlled Near-Singularity Diagnostic

To separate representation effects from rapid physical motion, an additional sequence holds the object approximately stationary with the measured middle XYZ Euler coordinate close to the singular configuration. A control sequence holds the object at an orientation farther from the singularity.

These values describe variation of the XYZ Euler coordinates; they do not mean that the physical object orientation itself oscillates by \(22^\circ\). The purpose of the diagnostic is to expose the poor conditioning of the Euler parameterization near its singular configuration.

Comparison with the \(SE(3)\) Error-State Formulation

A component-wise comparison of \(r_x\), \(r_y\), and \(r_z\) is not appropriate for the \(SE(3)\) ES-EKF because its rotational error state is not an XYZ Euler-angle state. Instead, the common quantity of interest is the local rotational discrepancy between the predicted and observed orientations. The \(SE(3)\) ES-EKF computes

Result: the controlled experiment shows that the near-\(90^\circ\) Euler singularity produces large, strongly coupled coordinate variation that propagates into the Euler KF innovation, corrected state, angular-velocity state, and subsequent prediction. In the independent \(SE(3)\) recording, the rotational innovation remains a small local Lie-algebra discrepancy. The dynamic trials further show that the Euler recordings can exhibit very large rotational residuals and output-pose transients in this regime, whereas tracking was maintained in the tested \(SE(3)\) sequences.

Main Finding

  • Comparable accuracy under smooth motion. With ground truth available in simulation, the Euler-angle KF and the proposed \(SE(3)\) ES-EKF achieve nearly identical pose accuracy.
  • Euler branch-boundary effect. At the \(\pm180^\circ\) Euler branch boundary, the Euler-angle KF can produce artificial near-\(360^\circ\) coordinate residuals even though the physical orientation remains continuous.
  • Near-singularity behaviour. As the middle XYZ Euler coordinate \(r_y\) approaches \(\pm90^\circ\), strongly coupled Euler-coordinate variation can propagate through the innovation, corrected state, angular-velocity state, and subsequent prediction.
  • Benefit of the \(SE(3)\) formulation. The \(SE(3)\) ES-EKF represents rotational disagreement as a local Lie-algebra error, avoiding direct subtraction of wrapped or near-singular Euler coordinates. Its main benefit is therefore geometric consistency and representation robustness rather than a universal reduction in tracking error.

Limitations & Future Work

  • Accurate ground-truth poses are available only in simulation. The offline LEGO and real-world ZED experiments therefore rely primarily on qualitative tracking behaviour and internal filter diagnostics.
  • The real-world stress tests contain a limited number of objects, sequences, and independently recorded trials. They characterize the observed behaviour of the two formulations but do not constitute a frame-synchronized accuracy benchmark.
  • The near-singularity experiments isolate effects associated with the pose representation, but tracking failure in the complete vision pipeline can also depend on image quality, occlusion, object motion, depth measurements, and FoundationPose estimation errors.
  • Future work should include externally measured real-world ground truth, synchronized repeated trajectories, and a broader range of objects and motion conditions.
