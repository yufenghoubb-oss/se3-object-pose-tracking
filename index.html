Reformulating the temporal filtering component of FoundationPose++ as an error-state Extended Kalman Filter on \(SE(3)\).

Abstract

Accurate and robust 6D object pose tracking is essential for robotic manipulation in dynamic environments. FoundationPose provides accurate object pose estimation and tracking through iterative pose refinement. However, when the target undergoes fast motion, a poor pose initialization may require additional refinement iterations, increasing computational cost, while insufficient refinement can lead to tracking failure.

FoundationPose++ addresses this problem by introducing a Kalman-filter-based motion prediction mechanism that combines pose observations with motion predictions to provide improved initialization for FoundationPose. In addition, a 2D tracker is incorporated to correct the predicted object location in the image plane.

The original FoundationPose++ filtering framework represents 3D orientation using Euler angles. In this project, the filter is reformulated as an error-state Extended Kalman Filter (ES-EKF) on \(SE(3)\). The nominal object pose is maintained directly on \(SE(3)\), while local pose uncertainty and Kalman corrections are represented in the corresponding Lie algebra. The original 2D tracking and measurement-fusion strategy is retained and integrated into the new error-state formulation.

Motivation

During continuous tracking, FoundationPose uses the pose estimated in the previous frame as the initialization for refinement on the current RGB-D observation. This strategy works well when inter-frame motion is relatively small.

For rapidly moving objects, however, the previous pose may differ significantly from the current pose. The resulting poor initialization makes pose refinement more difficult and may require additional refinement iterations. If sufficient refinement cannot be performed within the available processing time, tracking may fail.

Goal: provide FoundationPose with a temporally predicted and measurement-corrected pose initialization while representing rigid-body motion consistently with the geometry of \(SE(3)\).

FoundationPose++

FoundationPose++ combines FoundationPose with a linear Kalman Filter and a 2D object tracker to improve pose initialization during continuous tracking. FoundationPose represents the object pose as a rigid transformation matrix, whereas the original Kalman Filter operates on a Euclidean state composed of translation, XYZ Euler angles, and their corresponding rates.

After initialization, each tracking iteration consists of four main stages:

6D pose update  →  2D tracker update  →  FoundationPose refinement  →  motion prediction

Original State Representation

The original Kalman Filter uses the following 12-dimensional Euclidean state:

\[ \mathbf{x}_k = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{p}_k \\ \boldsymbol{\theta}_k \\ \mathbf{v}_k \\ \dot{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_k \end{bmatrix} \in \mathbb{R}^{12}. \]

Here, \(\mathbf{p}_k\) denotes translation, \(\boldsymbol{\theta}_k\) denotes XYZ Euler angles, \(\mathbf{v}_k\) denotes translational velocity, and \(\dot{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_k\) denotes the corresponding Euler-angle rates.

Limitations of Euler-Angle Filtering

Although the original linear Kalman Filter provides an efficient mechanism for temporal pose prediction, its formulation relies on a Euclidean representation of rigid-body pose. In particular, rotation is represented using XYZ Euler angles and processed in the same way as translation.

Euler angles are subject to parameterization singularities and angle periodicity. Directly subtracting two Euler-angle vectors does not in general provide a geometrically consistent measure of relative rotation. Furthermore, rotational motion is predicted by linear extrapolation of Euler-angle coordinates rather than by composition on the rotation group.

Three-dimensional rotations naturally belong to \(SO(3)\), while a complete rigid-body pose belongs to \(SE(3)\). This motivates a filtering formulation in which pose prediction, pose error, and correction are defined consistently with rigid-body geometry.

SE(3) Error-State EKF

The original filtering framework is reformulated as an error-state Extended Kalman Filter on \(SE(3)\). Instead of representing the complete pose as a Euclidean vector, the nominal object pose is maintained directly on \(SE(3)\), while local pose errors and Kalman corrections are represented in the corresponding Lie algebra.

Rigid-Body Pose

\[ \mathbf{T} = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{R} & \mathbf{p}\\ \mathbf{0}^{T} & 1 \end{bmatrix} \in SE(3). \]

The nominal state contains the rigid-body pose together with spatial linear and angular velocities:

\[ \mathcal{X}_k = \left( \mathbf{T}_k, \mathbf{v}_k, \boldsymbol{\omega}_k \right). \]

Local Error State

\[ \delta\mathbf{x}_k = \begin{bmatrix} \delta\boldsymbol{\rho}_k\\ \delta\boldsymbol{\phi}_k\\ \delta\mathbf{v}_k\\ \delta\boldsymbol{\omega}_k \end{bmatrix} \in\mathbb{R}^{12}. \]

A left-invariant pose error is adopted. The true pose is related to the nominal pose by

\[ \mathbf{T}_{k,\mathrm{true}} = \operatorname{Exp}_{SE(3)} \left( \delta\boldsymbol{\xi}_k \right) \mathbf{T}_k. \]
Key idea: the nominal rigid-body pose remains on \(SE(3)\). The EKF estimates only a local pose error in the tangent space, and the estimated correction is injected back onto the group through the exponential map.

Pose Correction on SE(3)

Let \(\mathbf{T}_{k}^{-}\) denote the predicted nominal pose and \(\mathbf{T}_{\mathrm{obs}}\) the pose measurement provided by FoundationPose. Instead of converting both rotations to Euler angles and subtracting their coordinates, the innovation is computed from their relative rigid-body transformation:

\[ \mathbf{r}_k = \operatorname{Log}_{SE(3)} \left( \mathbf{T}_{\mathrm{obs}} \left(\mathbf{T}_{k}^{-}\right)^{-1} \right). \]

The resulting pose correction is injected into the nominal state through the exponential map:

\[ \mathbf{T}_{k}^{+} = \operatorname{Exp}_{SE(3)} \left( \delta\hat{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_k \right) \mathbf{T}_{k}^{-}. \]

2D Tracker Update

The original 2D tracking measurement from FoundationPose++ is retained. The center of the tracked bounding box is back-projected into the camera coordinate system using the object depth and camera intrinsic parameters:

\[ x_k = \frac{(u_k-c_x)z}{f_x}, \qquad y_k = \frac{(v_k-c_y)z}{f_y}. \]

Under the adopted left-error representation, translation perturbation is coupled with rotation perturbation according to

\[ \delta\mathbf{p} \approx \delta\boldsymbol{\rho} - \mathbf{p}^{\wedge} \delta\boldsymbol{\phi}. \]

The measurement Jacobian is therefore reformulated so that the original image-plane measurement can be incorporated consistently into the \(SE(3)\) error-state formulation.

Motion Prediction on SE(3)

After FoundationPose refinement, the ES-EKF state is propagated using a constant spatial velocity model. Define

\[ \mathbf{u}_k = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{v}_k\\ \boldsymbol{\omega}_k \end{bmatrix}. \]

The nominal pose is propagated directly on \(SE(3)\):

\[ \mathbf{T}_{k+1}^{-} = \operatorname{Exp}_{SE(3)} \left( \mathbf{u}_k\Delta t_k \right) \mathbf{T}_{k}^{+}. \]

The covariance is propagated in local error coordinates. Under the left-error formulation, the error-state transition matrix is

\[ \mathbf{F}_k = \begin{bmatrix} \operatorname{Ad}_{\Delta\mathbf{T}_k} & \boldsymbol{\Gamma}_k\\ \mathbf{0}_{6} & \mathbf{I}_{6} \end{bmatrix}, \] \[ \boldsymbol{\Gamma}_k = \int_{0}^{\Delta t_k} \exp \left( \operatorname{ad}_{\mathbf{u}_k}s \right) ds. \]

The covariance is then propagated according to

\[ \mathbf{P}_{k+1}^{-} = \mathbf{F}_k \mathbf{P}_{k}^{+} \mathbf{F}_k^{T} + \mathbf{Q}_k. \]

Experiments and Results

The proposed formulation is evaluated in three settings: simulated robotic manipulation with ground truth, an offline RGB-D image sequence containing relatively rapid object motion, and a real-time ZED RGB-D tracking experiment.

Experiment I — Robot Manipulation Tracking in Simulation

A dexterous robotic hand manipulates a cube in the MuJoCo simulation environment while a fixed virtual RGB-D camera records a sequence of 504 frames. The cube is partially occluded by the hand for short periods during manipulation.

Ground-truth object poses are available for every frame. The same RGB-D sequence is processed using the original Euler-angle KF and the proposed \(SE(3)\) ES-EKF. Cutie, FoundationPose refinement, input data, and the remaining parameters are kept identical.

Experiment II — RGB-D Image-Sequence Tracking

The second experiment uses a 116-frame offline RGB-D sequence of a textured LEGO block. Compared with Experiment I, the sequence contains faster motion and larger inter-frame pose changes.

The same sequence is processed using the Euler-angle KF and the proposed \(SE(3)\) ES-EKF. Cutie is used as the 2D tracker in both configurations, while the remaining tracking components and parameters are kept unchanged.

Ground-truth poses are not available for this sequence. The evaluation therefore focuses on qualitative tracking behavior rather than quantitative pose accuracy.

Experiment III — Real-Time Tracking

The third experiment evaluates the tracking system in a real-world real-time scenario using a ZED RGB-D camera. A mustard bottle is manually moved in front of the camera, including relatively rapid side-to-side motions.

Three configurations are evaluated: FoundationPose alone, FoundationPose with the original Euler-angle KF, and FoundationPose with the proposed \(SE(3)\) ES-EKF.

For the two filtering-based configurations, OSTrack is used to provide an additional 2D image-plane measurement. In the real-time setup, OSTrack requires approximately 10 ms per frame, compared with approximately 33 ms per frame for Cutie.

During relatively slow object motion, all three configurations are able to estimate the object pose. During faster motion, FoundationPose alone becomes more sensitive to the initialization from the previous frame. Both filtering-based configurations use temporal motion prediction together with the 2D tracker measurement to provide a corrected initialization for FoundationPose.

Discussion

Experiment I shows that the original Euler-angle KF and the proposed \(SE(3)\) ES-EKF achieve nearly identical quantitative pose accuracy under relatively smooth and continuous motion. Therefore, reformulating the filter on \(SE(3)\) does not necessarily produce lower pose estimation errors under nominal motion conditions.

Experiment II considers faster motion and larger inter-frame pose changes. Nevertheless, both filtering configurations maintain continuous tracking, and no evident qualitative difference is observed under the tested conditions. Since ground-truth poses are unavailable, this experiment does not provide a quantitative comparison of pose accuracy.

Although Experiments I and II do not demonstrate a clear performance advantage of the \(SE(3)\) ES-EKF over the Euler-angle KF, the two formulations differ fundamentally in how rigid-body motion is represented. The original formulation performs rotational prediction and correction directly on Euler-angle coordinates. In the proposed formulation, the nominal pose remains on \(SE(3)\), pose differences are computed from relative rigid-body transformations using the logarithmic map, and corrections are applied through the exponential map.

Experiment III further demonstrates the practical role of temporal filtering in the complete real-time tracking pipeline. Both filtering-based configurations use the estimated motion state and additional 2D tracking measurement to provide a corrected initialization for FoundationPose.

Main finding: the contribution of the proposed \(SE(3)\) ES-EKF is not a universal improvement in pose accuracy or tracking robustness. Instead, it provides a geometrically consistent formulation of rigid-body pose prediction, pose error, and state correction while preserving the original FoundationPose++ motion-prediction and 2D measurement-fusion strategy.

Limitations and Future Work

The current experiments cover only a limited number of objects and motion sequences. Furthermore, accurate ground-truth poses are available only for the simulation experiment, limiting quantitative evaluation of the offline RGB-D and real-time experiments.

A more comprehensive evaluation should include additional target objects, systematically controlled translational and rotational velocities, larger inter-frame rotations, and more challenging motion patterns.

Accurate external ground-truth pose measurements in real-world experiments would enable a systematic quantitative comparison of the Euler-angle KF and the proposed \(SE(3)\) ES-EKF under different motion conditions.

Conclusion

This project reformulates the temporal filtering component of FoundationPose++ as an error-state Extended Kalman Filter on \(SE(3)\). Rather than directly filtering Euler-angle coordinates, the nominal rigid-body pose is maintained on \(SE(3)\), while local pose errors and corrections are represented in the corresponding Lie algebra.

Pose prediction is performed through the \(SE(3)\) exponential map, while pose residuals are computed from relative rigid-body transformations using the logarithmic map. The original 2D tracker measurement is retained and incorporated consistently with the adopted left-error representation.

Experiments show that the proposed formulation maintains tracking performance comparable to the original Euler-angle KF under the tested conditions. The principal contribution is therefore not a universal improvement in pose accuracy, but a geometrically consistent reformulation of the FoundationPose++ temporal filtering framework for rigid-body motion.
