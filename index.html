Reformulating the FoundationPose++ temporal filtering framework with an SE(3) error-state Extended Kalman Filter for geometrically consistent 6D object pose tracking.

Abstract

Accurate and robust 6D object pose tracking is important for robotic manipulation in dynamic environments. FoundationPose provides accurate pose estimation and tracking through iterative pose refinement, but rapid inter-frame motion can make the previous-frame pose a poor initialization. FoundationPose++ addresses this problem by introducing Kalman-filter-based motion prediction together with an additional 2D tracker.

The original FoundationPose++ filter represents 3D orientation using XYZ Euler angles and performs prediction and correction directly in Euclidean coordinates. In this project, the filtering component is reformulated as an error-state Extended Kalman Filter on \(SE(3)\). The nominal rigid-body pose remains directly on \(SE(3)\), while local pose errors and Kalman corrections are represented in the corresponding Lie algebra. The original 2D tracking and measurement-fusion strategy is retained and integrated into the new error-state formulation.

Experiments in MuJoCo simulation, an offline rapid-motion RGB-D sequence, and a real-time ZED-camera setup show that the two formulations perform similarly under smooth motion, while the proposed SE(3) formulation maintains robust tracking in the tested rapid-motion sequence and can be integrated into real-time RGB-D tracking.

Tracking Fast-Moving Objects

FoundationPose tracks an object by using the pose estimated in the previous frame as the initialization for the current RGB-D frame, followed by iterative refinement. This works well when inter-frame motion is small. With rapid motion, however, the previous pose may already be far from the current pose, making refinement more difficult and increasing the risk of tracking failure.

Key challenge: How can we provide FoundationPose with a better pose initialization when the object moves rapidly between consecutive frames?

FoundationPose++ and Temporal Motion Prediction

FoundationPose++ augments FoundationPose with a linear Kalman Filter and a 2D object tracker. The filter predicts the object motion over time, while the 2D tracker provides an additional image-plane localization measurement. Together, they provide a corrected initialization before FoundationPose refinement.

After initialization, every tracking iteration follows the same four-stage structure: 6D pose update → 2D tracker update → FoundationPose refinement → motion prediction.

Original state representation

The original filter uses a 12-dimensional Euclidean state containing translation, XYZ Euler angles, translational velocity, and Euler-angle rates:

\[ \mathbf{x}_k = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{p}_k & \boldsymbol{\theta}_k & \mathbf{v}_k & \dot{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_k \end{bmatrix}^{T}. \]

Why Move Away from Euler Angles?

Euler angles are convenient, but they are not a globally consistent representation of 3D rotation. They are affected by parameterization singularities and angle periodicity, and directly subtracting two Euler-angle vectors does not generally produce a geometrically consistent relative rotation. Likewise, linearly extrapolating Euler coordinates is different from composing rotations on the rotation group.

Three-dimensional rotations naturally belong to \(SO(3)\), while a full rigid-body pose belongs to \(SE(3)\). This motivates keeping the pose on the rigid-body transformation group instead of treating it as a six-dimensional Euclidean vector.

Euler-angle KF

Pose is represented as translation + Euler angles and manipulated directly in Euclidean coordinates.

SE(3) ES-EKF

The nominal pose remains on \(SE(3)\); only local errors and corrections live in a Euclidean tangent space.

Moving the Filter onto SE(3)

A rigid-body pose is represented directly as a homogeneous transformation:

\[ \mathbf{T} = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{R} & \mathbf{p}\\ \mathbf{0}^{T} & 1 \end{bmatrix} \in SE(3). \]

The nominal state contains the pose together with spatial linear and angular velocities. The filter does not estimate the full pose additively. Instead, it estimates a local 12-dimensional error state whose first six components form a pose perturbation \(\delta\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\mathfrak{se}(3)\).

Key idea: the nominal pose stays on \(SE(3)\), while the EKF estimates a small local error in the Lie algebra and injects that correction back onto the group.
\[ \mathbf{T}_{\mathrm{true}} = \operatorname{Exp}_{SE(3)}(\delta\boldsymbol{\xi})\,\mathbf{T}. \]

Prediction and Correction Without Euler Angles

6D pose correction

Instead of converting two rotations into Euler angles and subtracting them, the pose residual is computed from the relative rigid-body transformation:

\[ \mathbf{r}_k = \operatorname{Log}_{SE(3)} \left( \mathbf{T}_{\mathrm{obs}} (\mathbf{T}_{k}^{-})^{-1} \right). \]

The resulting EKF correction is injected into the nominal pose through the exponential map:

\[ \mathbf{T}_{k}^{+} = \operatorname{Exp}_{SE(3)} \left( \delta\hat{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_k \right) \mathbf{T}_{k}^{-}. \]

Motion prediction

Under the constant spatial velocity model, the pose is propagated directly on \(SE(3)\):

\[ \mathbf{T}_{k+1}^{-} = \operatorname{Exp}_{SE(3)} \left( \mathbf{u}_k\Delta t_k \right) \mathbf{T}_{k}^{+}, \qquad \mathbf{u}_k = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{v}_k\\ \boldsymbol{\omega}_k \end{bmatrix}. \]

2D tracker update

The 2D tracking measurement from FoundationPose++ is retained. The bounding-box center is back-projected into camera coordinates and used as an additional measurement. Its Jacobian is reformulated so that the update is consistent with the adopted left-error \(SE(3)\) representation.

Experiments

The proposed formulation was evaluated in three settings: simulated robotic manipulation with ground truth, an offline rapid-motion RGB-D sequence, and a real-time ZED RGB-D tracking experiment.

Experiment 1 — Robot Manipulation in MuJoCo

A dexterous robotic hand manipulates a cube in MuJoCo while a fixed virtual RGB-D camera records 504 frames. Ground-truth object poses are available for every frame. The original Euler-angle KF and the proposed SE(3) ES-EKF are evaluated using the same RGB-D input, Cutie tracker, FoundationPose refinement, and parameters.

Experiment 2 — Rapid-Motion RGB-D Tracking

The second experiment uses a 116-frame offline RGB-D sequence of a textured LEGO block with faster motion and larger inter-frame pose changes. Both configurations use Cutie and the same FoundationPose tracking pipeline. Evaluation is qualitative because no external ground-truth trajectory is available.

Both methods track successfully in the earlier part of the sequence. Around frame 88, the Euler-angle KF configuration begins to deviate from the object and subsequently loses accurate tracking, while the SE(3) ES-EKF configuration maintains continuous tracking over the shown interval.

Experiment 3 — Real-Time RGB-D Tracking

A mustard bottle is tracked online using a ZED RGB-D camera while undergoing slow and relatively rapid side-to-side motion. Three configurations are compared: FoundationPose only, FoundationPose with the original Euler-angle KF, and FoundationPose with the proposed SE(3) ES-EKF.

OSTrack is used as the 2D tracker in the real-time experiment because it runs at approximately 10 ms per frame in this setup, compared with approximately 33 ms per frame for Cutie. During slow motion, all three configurations track successfully. During faster motion, FoundationPose alone becomes less stable, while both filtering-based configurations maintain more consistent tracking.

What Did We Learn?

The experiments show that replacing an Euler-angle filter with an SE(3) error-state formulation does not automatically reduce tracking error. Under smooth motion, the original formulation already works well and the measured accuracy is almost identical.

The main contribution is instead a geometrically consistent formulation of rigid-body prediction, pose error, and correction. The nominal pose remains on \(SE(3)\), pose differences are obtained from relative rigid-body transformations through the logarithmic map, and corrections are injected through the exponential map.

The rapid-motion RGB-D experiment provides the clearest observed difference: the SE(3) configuration maintains tracking in the tested challenging interval after the Euler-angle configuration begins to deviate. The real-time experiment also confirms that the proposed filter can be integrated into the complete FoundationPose + 2D tracker pipeline without losing the practical robustness benefit of temporal motion prediction.

Limitations and Future Work

The current evaluation covers only a limited number of objects and motion sequences. A more comprehensive study should include systematically controlled translational and rotational velocities, larger inter-frame rotations, more object geometries, and additional occlusion conditions.

Accurate external ground-truth pose measurements in the real-world experiments would also enable quantitative comparison of the two filtering formulations under different motion regimes.

Conclusion

This project reformulates the temporal filtering component of FoundationPose++ as an SE(3) error-state Extended Kalman Filter. Rather than filtering Euler-angle coordinates directly, the nominal rigid-body pose is kept on \(SE(3)\), with local uncertainty and corrections represented in the corresponding Lie algebra.

The proposed formulation preserves the original motion-prediction and 2D measurement-fusion strategy. Experiments indicate that both formulations can perform similarly under nominal motion, while the SE(3) formulation provides geometrically consistent rigid-body filtering and shows improved tracking robustness in the tested rapid-motion sequence.
